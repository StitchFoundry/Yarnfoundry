# YarnFoundry roadmap

## Honest assessment
Pre-alpha (2026-11-09). The core works headlessly: gauge math, `.yarnfoundry` files, picture to chart, words to chart, technique rules and written instructions, all tested. The app can open a chart and draw it, but there is printable charts (PDF/SVG/PNG) work, with a guided five-step setup and a browser build (publishing waits for the Pages workflow to be reviewed and enabled).
Self-assessed, measured by behaviour: the gaps below are NOT done.

## Progress log
- 2026-11-09 YF-15 3D preview: `ui-egui::figure3d` shows the original model beside the crocheted result (every round a ring of the right size and height, coloured by part, every 5th round lighter), drag to turn, scroll to zoom, spin, click a part to show only it; `yarnfoundry --model sample:snowman|file.stl --height 6` opens it; `render::view3d` projects both views (rotation-independent fit, large models drawn with a triangle cap). Honest note: it is drawn through egui's painter from CPU-projected polygons, not a custom wgpu pipeline (so it works in the same way in the browser build, which is checked by `cargo xtask wasm`; the browser page itself is not yet wired to open a model). Also: the sample bear (`models::bear`) and `models::sample(name)`. Checked: hostile cameras, both views draw, size does not change while turning. Limit: the bear's shoulders flare where arm lobes join the body. Not done: wizard path and printable pattern (YF-16).
- 2026-11-09 YF-14 parts: `core::parts` follows how the loops of each slice join and separate (a limb is its own loop, then joins the body) and cuts there, plus cuts a single chain at a neck (perimeter under 60% of the smaller bulge each side); manual split planes (add a height, remove an automatic neck cut); each part gets its own rounds, starting at a closed tip where there is one (arms and heads are worked from the top); the widest part is the Body, others named Head / Left arm / Right leg / ear by position; attachment notes ('Sew the open end of the left arm to the body between Rnd 12 and Rnd 13'); per-part patterns. Checked: the snowman splits into 2 parts at the neck, a body with four slanting limbs splits into body + 4 limbs (all attached to the body), ball/capsule/cylinder/cone stay one part. `models::sticks` (marching tetrahedra) builds closed unions of balls and limbs. `yarnfoundry-cli pattern` now prints every part. Limits: cuts are horizontal planes, so a limb that grows sideways from the body is cut at the height where its loop separates, and slanted limbs get slightly large counts. Not done: 3D preview in the app, wizard, PDF (YF-15, YF-16).
- 2026-11-09 YF-13 written amigurumi pattern: `core::pattern` writes `Rnd 1: 6 sc in magic ring (6)`, `Rnd 2: inc x6 (12)`, `Rnd 3: (sc, inc) x6 (18)`, `Rnd 8: sc 36 (36)`; increases and decreases spread evenly (even gaps) and every other changing round starts half a gap later so the piece does not turn hexagonal; US terms or UK terms; stuffing start, safety-eye round from a marked point on the model (or a default), fasten-off lines, abbreviations key, totals and a yardage estimate (factor per yarn weight, +10%, labelled an estimate). Checked: a test reads every written line back and confirms it uses and makes the right stitch counts for every round change from 6 to 60. `yarnfoundry-cli pattern`. Not done: parts, preview, PDF (YF-14 to YF-16).
- 2026-11-09 YF-12 rounds: `core::slice` cuts a closed mesh into horizontal loops (our own plain-geometry method); `core::rounds` places rounds one row-height apart along the surface, sets stitches = perimeter / stitch width, then smooths so every round can be made from the one before (magic ring of 6, at most +/-6 a round, never below 6, closes at 6). Checked: a ball sized for 36 stitches gives 6, 12, 18, 23, 28, 31, 34, 36, 36, 36, 34 ... 6 (within one of the circle's own counts), a cylinder holds steady, property tests that every round is buildable (increases use one stitch, decreases two). `yarnfoundry-cli rounds`. Not done: written pattern, parts, preview (YF-13 to YF-16). Limit: slices are horizontal, so a limb that is not roughly vertical gets slightly large counts.
